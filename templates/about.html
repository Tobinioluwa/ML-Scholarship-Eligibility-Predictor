About the Model

The Data — Real, Not Fabricated

This model is trained on real student records, not synthetic/generated data. Two genuine, publicly available datasets were used:

Dataset Role Size Source & Download
UCI Student Performance Primary training data 395 real students UCI Repository page · direct CSV
Graduate Admission Prediction Supplementary reference only 400 real applicants Kaggle page · direct CSV

Both raw files are committed unmodified in this repository at data/raw/, with full citations in data/raw/SOURCES.md.

Why the Eligibility Label Had to Be Derived

No public dataset records real students' actual scholarship decisions — that's sensitive financial and personal information tied to real people, so it isn't released anywhere openly (we checked; the only public "scholarship datasets" are lists of scholarship programs, not applicant-level outcomes). So while every row here is a real student, the eligible label is computed with a fully transparent, deterministic rule (no randomness) applied to that student's real attributes — it is not an actual historical committee decision. Treat this project's accuracy numbers as a demonstration of the ML pipeline, not a validated real-world policy.

How Eligibility Is Derived

Computed once, per real student, in src/prepare_dataset.py:

  • Merit score (55% weight): a weighted average of the student's three real grade periods (20% + 30% + 50%, recent grades weighted more), minus a penalty per past class failure, plus a small bonus for weekly study time.
  • Need score (35% weight): higher when parents' average education level is lower, when there's no home internet access, when the student doesn't receive paid tutoring, when household size is larger, and when there's no family educational support — all real fields, used as standard socioeconomic proxies.
  • Small bonuses (10% weight): having extracurricular activities, and below-median absences.
  • The top ~40% of students by combined score are labeled eligible = 1.

Columns unrelated to scholarship eligibility (alcohol consumption, romantic relationship status, going-out frequency, specific school name) were deliberately excluded from both the label formula and the model's inputs, even though they exist in the real dataset.

Limitations
  • The "eligible" label is a documented derived rule, not a real committee's historical decision — no such public dataset exists.
  • Only 395 training rows from two Portuguese secondary schools — results won't generalize to other regions, education systems, or scholarship programs without retraining on local data.
  • Because the label is partly a function of grades, and grades are also model inputs, reported accuracy is optimistic relative to a real, independently-labeled dataset.
  • To use your own institution's real historical data, replace data/raw/student-mat.csv (keeping equivalent columns) and re-run src/prepare_dataset.py then src/train_model.py.
  • Always pair automated screening with human review before final decisions.
